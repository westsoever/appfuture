The lateral hypothalamic area (LHA) is a small but powerful hub deep in the brain that drives feeding behavior, food-seeking motivation, and reward — it contains specialized neurons (like orexin/hypocretin and MCH cells) that essentially shout "go find and eat something rewarding" when activated, and it's a key node linking hunger to the dopamine reward system. The observed ~21% drop in LHA activity (though statistically weak here, p=0.55, meaning we can't rule out chance) is directionally consistent with the idea that Semaglutide suppresses appetite not just by acting on classic "fullness" centers like the brainstem, but by quieting the brain circuits that generate the *drive* and *wanting* for food.
